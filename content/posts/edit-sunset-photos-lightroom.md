**To edit a sunset photo, recover the highlights, lift the shadows, warm the white balance, boost vibrance rather than saturation, deepen the oranges and reds in the HSL panel, and finish with a soft graduated filter over the sky.** That sequence turns a flat, phone-flattened sky into the glowing scene you actually saw. Here's the full walk-through — it works the same in Lightroom on desktop and mobile.

## Step by step

1. **Start from RAW.** RAW files hold far more colour and detail. If you shot JPEG, this still works — you just have less room.
2. **Fix the exposure.** Set overall brightness first so the rest of the sliders behave.
3. **Highlights down, Shadows up.** Pull **Highlights** left to recover the bright sky near the sun, and push **Shadows** right to open up the dark foreground.
4. **Warm the white balance.** Nudge **Temp** toward yellow and **Tint** slightly toward magenta to bring back the golden tones auto white balance stripped out.
5. **Vibrance over Saturation.** Add **Vibrance** for rich colour that doesn't go cartoonish. Use Saturation sparingly.
6. **Shape colour in HSL.** In the **HSL/Color** panel, boost the **luminance/saturation of oranges and reds**, and drag the blues a touch darker for contrast.
7. **Graduated filter on the sky.** Drag a **graduated filter** down from the top and slightly lower its exposure while adding warmth — this deepens the sky without darkening the land.
8. **Final polish.** A little **Contrast**, a **Clarity/Texture** nudge on the clouds, and a gentle **vignette** to draw the eye. Don't overdo it.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Over-saturating** until the sky looks fake — pull it back until it looks like the moment you remember.
- **Crushing the shadows** to pure black — keep some detail in the foreground.
- **Ignoring white balance** — this is the single biggest fix for a "flat" sunset.
- **One edit for every photo** — presets are a starting point; always tweak exposure and white balance per shot.
