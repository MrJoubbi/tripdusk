## What is an eSIM, and why does it matter for long-term travel?

An eSIM is a digital SIM card built into your phone. Instead of inserting a physical card, you scan a QR code and a new mobile plan activates instantly. For someone travelling for weeks or months, that's a genuine quality-of-life upgrade:

- **Online the moment you land** — set it up before you fly and it connects on arrival.
- **Keep your home number** — the eSIM runs alongside your normal SIM, so you still get calls and bank texts.
- **Switch countries painlessly** — buy a new plan from the app instead of finding a local shop each time.
- **No deposit, no contract** — you pay only for the data you need.

## How do I choose the right eSIM for a long trip?

Run through this quick checklist before you buy:

- **How many countries?** One country → a local plan or Holafly. A whole region → Airalo or Nomad regional. Many regions → a global plan.
- **How much data?** Light (maps, messaging) ≈ 1 GB/week. Heavy (video calls, hotspot) → an unlimited plan.
- **Do you tether a laptop?** Confirm the plan allows hotspot — most do, but a few unlimited plans throttle it.
- **Top-up or re-buy?** For long trips, a toppable eSIM (Airalo, Nomad) saves reinstalling.
- **Network quality.** Check which local networks the plan uses; the cheapest isn't always the fastest.

## How to install an eSIM in 5 minutes

The process is nearly identical on iPhone and Android:

1. **Check compatibility.** Your phone must be eSIM-capable and carrier-unlocked (most phones from 2019 on qualify).
2. **Buy a plan** in the provider's app — you'll get a QR code by email or in-app.
3. **Add the eSIM.** iPhone: `Settings → Cellular → Add eSIM`. Android: `Settings → Network → SIMs → Add eSIM`. Scan the QR code.
4. **Set your data line** to the new eSIM, and keep your home SIM for calls/texts.
5. **Turn on Data Roaming for the eSIM line only** — required for it to connect abroad, and it won't trigger home-carrier charges.

<div class="callout"><p><strong>Tip:</strong> install the eSIM while you still have Wi-Fi at home or your hotel — you need a connection to activate it. Activate the <em>plan</em> when you land so the validity window doesn't start early.</p></div>

## eSIM vs local SIM vs pocket Wi-Fi

Which is right depends on how you travel:

- **eSIM** — fastest and simplest; best for arrivals, short stays, and frequent border crossings.
- **Local SIM** — usually the cheapest per GB for a long stay in one country, but means a shop visit and sometimes ID registration.
- **Pocket Wi-Fi** — useful only if you travel as a group sharing one connection, or need a phone with no eSIM support.

Most long-term travelers we know use an eSIM by default and pick up a local SIM only when they settle somewhere for a month or more.
