**To photograph a great sunset, use a low ISO (100–200), an aperture of f/8–f/11, a Cloudy or Shade white balance to keep the warm tones, and expose for the sky rather than the foreground — then keep shooting through the 20 minutes after the sun sets, when the colours are often best.** That's the short version. Below are the eight settings and habits that turn a flat, washed-out sunset into a glowing, sharp keeper — on a camera or a phone.

## The 8 settings that make a sunset sing

1. **Shoot in RAW.** RAW keeps far more colour and shadow detail, so you can rescue a sky that looked blown-out. Most cameras and recent phones support it.
2. **Keep ISO low (100–200).** Low ISO means clean, noise-free files. There's plenty of light at golden hour, so you rarely need more.
3. **Aperture f/8–f/11.** This is the sharpness sweet spot for most lenses and keeps both foreground and sky in focus.
4. **Expose for the sky.** Meter off the bright part of the sky (not the sun itself). A darker, richer sky beats a washed-out one — you can lift the foreground later.
5. **White balance: Cloudy or Shade.** Auto white balance often "corrects" the warmth away. Cloudy/Shade keeps the golds and oranges you actually saw.
6. **Use a low, interesting foreground.** A silhouette — a person, a tree, a rock — gives the scene depth and scale. Get low to exaggerate it.
7. **Steady the shot.** As light drops, shutter speeds slow. A small tripod (or bracing on a wall) keeps things sharp. <a href="#" rel="sponsored nofollow">See travel tripods →</a>
8. **Bracket your exposures.** Take one at the metered exposure, one darker, one brighter. You'll almost always prefer one you didn't expect.

## Golden hour vs blue hour — know the difference

- **Golden hour** — the ~30 minutes before sunset, when light is warm and directional. Best for silhouettes and warm portraits.
- **Blue hour** — the ~20–30 minutes *after* the sun dips below the horizon, when the sky turns deep blue and pink. Often the richest colours of the day — so don't pack up when the sun's gone.

## How to shoot a great sunset on your phone

No camera? Your phone is more than enough:

- **Tap the sky** to set focus and exposure, then **drag down** to darken slightly so colours stay saturated.
- **Turn on RAW / ProRAW** if available, and edit later for the best results.
- **Skip digital zoom** — it just crops and softens. Move your feet instead.
- **Lock it steady** — lean on something or use a mini tripod for the low-light moments.
- **Turn off HDR** if it's flattening the drama; sometimes the "worse" exposure is the better photo.

The best sunset photo is mostly about *being there, ready*. Scout your spot early, get low, expose for the sky, and keep shooting past the sunset into blue hour.
