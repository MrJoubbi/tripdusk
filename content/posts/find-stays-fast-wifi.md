**To find a stay with genuinely fast Wi-Fi, make the host prove it with a speed-test screenshot, read recent reviews for the word 'Wi-Fi', and always carry a mobile-data backup so one bad router never costs you a workday.** Here's the routine.

## Before you book

- **Ask for a real number.** "Fast Wi-Fi" is meaningless — request a **speed-test screenshot** showing download *and* upload. Aim for 25+ Mbps down, 5+ up.
- **Read recent reviews** for "Wi-Fi", "internet" or "work" — recent, specific mentions from other remote workers are gold; vague or old ones aren't.
- **Ask about the setup** — is it fibre or mobile? One router or mesh? Any data cap?
- **Check the neighbourhood** — rural and island stays can have slower or capped connections.

## Your backup plan

- **A travel eSIM** for hotspotting when the Wi-Fi dies — see our <a href="best-esims-long-term-travel.html">best eSIMs guide</a>.
- **A travel router** to turn any connection into your own secure network — see the <a href="remote-work-travel-kit.html">remote-work travel kit</a>.
- **A nearby coworking space or café** identified before you arrive.
