**Our travel camera kit is deliberately small: one mirrorless body, a versatile zoom plus a fast prime, a compact travel tripod, spare batteries and a fast card — everything in a packable bag.** You don't need more than this to shoot great travel and sunset photos, and carrying less means you'll actually use it. Here's the full kit, plus a phone-only version.

## The kit

- **A mirrorless body** — the best balance of quality, size and price. A mid-range crop or full-frame body is plenty for travel. <a href="#" rel="sponsored nofollow">See travel cameras →</a>
- **A versatile zoom (e.g. 24–105mm equivalent)** — one lens for landscapes, streets and details, so you rarely swap in the field.
- **A fast prime (35mm or 50mm, f/1.8)** — for low light, blue hour, and portraits with lovely background blur.
- **A compact travel tripod** — essential as the light drops; keeps sunset and blue-hour shots sharp. <a href="#" rel="sponsored nofollow">See travel tripods →</a>
- **Spare batteries + a fast SD card** — cold evenings drain batteries; sunsets fill cards.
- **A packable, padded bag** — protects the kit and disappears into your luggage.

## Nice-to-haves

- **ND and graduated ND filters** — smooth long exposures and balance a bright sky. <a href="#" rel="sponsored nofollow">See filters →</a>
- **A remote or use the 2-second timer** — avoids camera shake on long exposures.
- **A microfibre cloth** — sea spray and dust are constant on sunset shoots.

## The phone-only alternative

You genuinely don't need a camera to start:

- **A recent phone shooting RAW/ProRAW** covers most scenes.
- **A mini/flexible tripod** for steady low-light shots.
- **A clip-on lens** if you want wider or more reach.

The settings and composition matter far more than the gear — see our <a href="how-to-photograph-sunset.html">guide to photographing the perfect sunset</a>.
